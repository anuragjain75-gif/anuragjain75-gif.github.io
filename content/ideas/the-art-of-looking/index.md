The world does not always reveal itself to those who merely glance at it. A tree, a moth, a stone lying beside a road—each may remain an insignificant object until something in the act of looking changes. Then the familiar becomes strange, and the ordinary acquires an almost inexhaustible depth.

To look is not simply to receive an image. It is to enter into a relationship with what is seen, allowing its details, textures and peculiarities to emerge from the background of familiarity.
