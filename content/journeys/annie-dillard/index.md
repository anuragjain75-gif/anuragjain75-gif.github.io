Annie Dillard's writing opens a passage into a world in which the natural landscape is at once extraordinarily beautiful and deeply unsettling. Her books bring us face to face with the abundance of life, its astonishing ingenuity, and the violence that accompanies its perpetual renewal.

This journey begins with *Pilgrim at Tinker Creek*, but it does not end with the book. It follows the questions that emerge from Dillard's encounters with nature: the relationship between beauty and cruelty, the problem of suffering, the place of human existence in the natural world, and the metaphysical assumptions through which we attempt to make sense of it all.

It is also an exploration of Dillard as a literary presence: her descriptive powers, her intellectual temperament, her relationship with religious thought, and the particular vision of existence that informs her writing.

The journey will unfold through books, ideas, literary encounters and personal reflections. Its purpose is not simply to arrive at an interpretation of Annie Dillard, but to preserve the paths of thought opened by an encounter with her work.
